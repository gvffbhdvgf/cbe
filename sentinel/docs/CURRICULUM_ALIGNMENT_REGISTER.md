# Sentinel — KICD Curriculum Alignment Register (Initial)

**Status:** Structural register only; outcome mapping is not approved  
**Reference date:** 25 November 2026  
**Scope:** Regular curriculum, Grades 1–9; assessment-report comparison examples remain Grades 6 and 9

## 1. Governing rule

Sentinel must store the official KICD curriculum as a versioned hierarchy. The report's locally constructed subject headings and composites are assessment constructs; they must not be treated as official curriculum learning areas unless a curriculum specialist confirms equivalence.

For each curriculum record, retain source authority and URL, curriculum edition/revision, grade and learning-area code/name, strand and sub-strand hierarchy, general and specific learning outcomes, suggested learning experiences, key inquiry questions, core competencies, values, PCIs, assessment rubric, source page/location, effective dates, supersession relationship, transcription/review status and reviewer.

## 2A. Catalogue coverage extension (Grades 1–9)

The live Sentinel curriculum registry now has a source-linked regular catalogue for every grade from 1 through 9. The current KICD pages list eight lower-primary learning-area designs for Grades 1–3, 15 upper-primary designs for Grades 4–6, and 16 junior-school designs for Grades 7–9. A separate Indigenous Language entry for Grades 1–3 is flagged as a source discrepancy because it appears in the September 2026 complementary-materials submission scope but not on the current Lower Primary design page. It is not treated as a confirmed design offering.

The SNE registry distinguishes HI, PI and VI adapted-design categories from framework modalities (regular with adaptations, accelerated/enriched, specialised and intervention) and from prevocational/vocational levels. Grade-level SNE offerings are not inferred from the index page; they require page-level document availability and content verification.

## 3. Assessment-report constructs that must remain distinct

The Grade 6 report uses aggregate columns including MATH, ENG, KISW, CA/ST/CRE and SCI & TECH / AGRI & NUTR.

The Grade 9 report uses MATH, ENG, KISW, S/ST, INT/SCI, C/A & SPORT, PRETECH, AGR/NU and C.R.E.

These headings may represent local examination groupings, combined papers or score composites. Sentinel must preserve the original source label as an assessment-specific construct and separately link it to one or more official curriculum learning areas only after documentary confirmation.

Do not rename an assessment composite as a KICD learning area without evidence, divide a composite score across multiple learning areas without item-level evidence, infer outcome mastery from a subject-level mean, infer that a learning area was assessed because it appears in the KICD catalogue, or assume all learners took the same optional languages or religious education.

## 4. Required evidence before outcome-level mapping

Obtain and review:
1. Actual Grade 6 and Grade 9 assessment papers, including sections and items.
2. Marking schemes, rubrics and performance-level conversion tables.
3. Assessment blueprints or tables of specification, if available.
4. Moderation records and approved subject-combination rules.
5. Curriculum Support Officer confirmation of curriculum edition and learning-area names.
6. Special-needs adaptations or approved accommodations relevant to participating learners.

Then create a mapping record for every question/task: assessment and instrument version; question/item identifier; source page; raw scoring rule and maximum score; official KICD curriculum version; grade and learning area; strand and sub-strand; specific learning outcome(s); rubric criterion/evidence descriptor; mapping rationale and confidence; reviewer and approval status.

A question may map to multiple outcomes where justified. A curriculum specialist must review and approve mappings before they contribute to outcome analytics.

## 5. Version and accessibility rules

- Maintain separate curriculum editions and do not overwrite superseded designs.
- Distinguish regular curriculum designs from Special Needs Education designs/adaptations.
- Model learner accommodations as assessment context, not lower expectations or inferred ability.
- Keep source wording alongside normalized labels.
- Provide explicit unmapped, candidate, reviewed and approved mapping states.
- Preserve an auditable history for every mapping change.
